# Administration of Personal Income Tax (Direct Assessment) for the Nigerian Gig Economy under NTA 2025

## Executive Summary & Legal Framework

The modern Nigerian gig economy—comprising freelancers, remote tech workers, digital content creators, influencers, and digital vendors—occupies a unique position in Nigeria's tax architecture. Under the **Nigeria Tax Act (NTA) 2025** (which entered into force on **January 1, 2026**), Personal Income Tax (PIT) administration underwent its most radical modernization since the enactment of the Personal Income Tax Act (PITA) 2011 (as amended).

For self-employed digital workers whose income is ascertainable via bank transfers, fintech wallets, and digital platforms (e.g., Upwork, Fiverr, Deel, YouTube, Stripe, Paystack), state tax compliance is executed strictly under the **Direct Assessment (Self-Assessment)** regime. Under Section 2 of PITA (retained and updated in NTA 2025), tax residency dictates jurisdiction: an individual who spends **183 days or more** in a state or has a principal place of residence therein pays PIT to that state's Internal Revenue Service (SIRS).

## 1. NTA 2025 Tax Bands, Allowable Deductions & Foreign Income Rules

### A. Progressive Tax Rates & Exemption Threshold
NTA 2025 repealed the old Consolidated Relief Allowance (CRA) structure (formerly ₦200,000 + 20% of gross income) and replaced it with a **₦800,000 tax-free annual threshold** alongside updated progressive tax bands:

| Annual Taxable Income Band | Tax Rate | Notes / Cumulative Burden |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **First ₦800,000** | **0%** | Tax-exempt base (Nil return still mandatory for TCC) |
| **Next ₦2,200,000** (₦800,001 – ₦3,000,000) | **15%** | First taxable bracket |
| **Next ₦9,000,000** (₦3,000,001 – ₦12,000,000) | **18%** | Middle-income bracket |
| **Next ₦13,000,000** (₦12,000,001 – ₦25,000,000) | **21%** | Upper middle-income bracket |
| **Next ₦25,000,000** (₦25,000,001 – ₦50,000,000) | **23%** | High-earner bracket |
| **Above ₦50,000,000** | **25%** | Top marginal rate |

### B. Section 20 Allowable Deductions for Digital Workers
Under Section 20 of the NTA 2025, self-employed taxpayers under Direct Assessment can deduct expenses **wholly, exclusively, necessarily, and reasonably incurred** in the generation of their taxable income.

1. **Home Office & Remote Work Expenses:**
   - **Rent Relief:** NTA 2025 introduced an evidence-backed rent relief deduction of **20% of annual rent paid**, subject to a maximum cap of **₦500,000 per annum** (requires tenancy agreement & payment receipts).
   - **Utilities & Power:** Proportional deduction for electricity bills, solar inverter maintenance, and generator fueling used specifically for work hours.
   - **Data & Telecommunications:** Internet subscription costs (fiber optic, Starlink, mobile data bundles) allocated to business operations.
2. **Tools of Trade & Depreciation:**
   - Work laptops, high-performance GPUs, monitors, cameras, lighting setups, and software subscriptions (SaaS tools like Figma, Adobe Creative Cloud, GitHub, AWS/GCP hosting, Zoom).
   - Repairs, maintenance, and capital allowance claims on work assets.
3. **Statutory Contributions & Professional Expenses:**
   - Voluntary Pension contributions (Pension Reform Act), National Housing Fund (NHF), and Health Maintenance Organization (HMO) / NHIS premiums.
   - Professional membership fees, online courses, and skill certifications directly tied to income generation.

### C. Foreign Currency Earnings & Worldwide Income
Under NTA 2025, Nigerian tax residents are taxed on their **worldwide income**. Remote workers earning in USD, EUR, GBP, or CAD via international platforms (Deel, Wise, Payoneer, Remote.com) must:
- Declare foreign earnings converted to Naira at the **official Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN) exchange rate** (or official NAFEX/I&E rate) prevailing on the date the payment was received.
- Claim Double Taxation Relief (DTR) under Section 27 where foreign tax was withheld at source in a treaty country.

## 2. State-by-State Filing Procedures & Digital Portals

### A. Lagos State Internal Revenue Service (LIRS) – *eTax Portal*
Lagos State houses the largest concentration of tech workers, creative freelancers, and digital agencies in West Africa.

* **Digital Portal:** **[eTax Portal (etax.lirs.net)](https://etax.lirs.net)**
* **Registration & ID:** Registration requires the individual's Bank Verification Number (BVN) and National Identification Number (NIN) to issue/link a unique **LIRS Taxpayer ID (Payer ID)**.
* **Direct Assessment Filing Workflow:**
  1. Log into eTax using Taxpayer ID and password.
  2. Navigate to **"File Annual Returns"** (Form A self-assessment under Section 41 PITA/NTA).
  3. Input total gross income (local and converted foreign streams).
  4. Deduct allowable Section 20 expenses and upload supporting schedules (Statement of Income & Expenditure).
  5. Upload 12-month bank statements, invoice logs, and payment receipts.
  6. The eTax portal automatically applies NTA 2025 tax brackets and generates an Assessment Notice with a payment reference code (Bill Reference).
  7. Pay via integrated channels (Quickteller, WebPAY, direct bank transfer).
* **Filing Deadline:** **March 31st** annually for the preceding tax year.
* **TCC Issuance:** Upon verification of receipt of full payment, an electronic **Tax Clearance Certificate (e-TCC)** is downloadable directly from the eTax dashboard.

### B. Federal Capital Territory Internal Revenue Service (FCT-IRS) – *FCT Taxportal*
FCT-IRS manages tax collection for residents in the Federal Capital Territory (Abuja and satellite towns).

* **Digital Portal:** **[FCT-IRS Self Service Portal (fcttaxportal.fctirs.gov.ng)](https://fcttaxportal.fctirs.gov.ng)**
* **Registration & ID:** Registration uses BVN/NIN verification to generate a unique **FCT Tax Identification Number (TIN)**.
* **Direct Assessment Filing Workflow:**
  1. Access the portal dashboard and select **Tax Returns -> File a New Return**.
  2. Generate a **Returns Reference Number (RRN)** for the assessment year.
  3. Fill out the digitized Form A (Parts A through D covering income, allowable deductions, dependents/rent relief, and net chargeable income).
  4. Declare any Withholding Tax (WHT) credits deducted by domestic clients (uploading WHT credit notes to offset PIT liability).
  5. Upload bank statements and expense proofs.
  6. Submit for automated self-assessment calculation and pay via Remita/FCT-IRS payment gateway.
* **Enforcement:** FCT-IRS enforces strict compliance for Abuja-based remote workers applying for government services, land allocation, or passport renewals, requiring an FCT-IRS TCC.

### C. Rivers State Internal Revenue Service (RIRS) – *RIVTAMIS Portal*
Rivers State (Port Harcourt tech & creative hub) utilizes a fully digitized revenue platform.

* **Digital Portal:** **[RIVTAMIS (rivtamis.riversbirs.gov.ng)](https://www.rivtamis.riversbirs.gov.ng)**
* **Registration & ID:** Users register as "Individual" using NIN/BVN to generate their **RIVTIN (Rivers State Tax Identification Number)**.
* **Direct Assessment Filing Workflow:**
  1. Log in to RIVTAMIS using RIVTIN and password.
  2. Select **Self-Assessment / New Declaration**.
  3. Enter gross income breakdown and itemized business expense deductions.
  4. Submit income statement and bank account summary.
  5. Obtain system-generated assessment bill and pay through online banking or designated partner banks in Port Harcourt.
  6. Declare payment reference on the portal to instantly generate e-Receipts and trigger TCC processing.

## 3. Presumptive Tax vs. Direct Assessment: The Gig Economy Audit Realities

State Revenue Services strictly segregate self-employed individuals into two tax regimes:

| Feature | Presumptive Tax Regime | Direct Assessment (Self-Assessment) Regime |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Target Demographic** | Informal, undocumented micro-traders, artisans, street vendors with no formal bank records or accounting systems. | **Gig economy workers, freelancers, remote tech workers, digital content creators, online vendors.** |
| **Tax Base** | Standardized lump-sum or flat **1% turnover tax** based on estimated trade bands. | Actual net taxable profit (**Gross Income minus Section 20 Allowable Expenses**). |
| **Record Keeping** | Minimal or none required. | Mandatory (bank statements, invoices, expense receipts, contract agreements). |
| **Tax Rate** | Presumed flat/band rate. | **NTA 2025 Progressive Bands (0% to 25%)**. |

### The "Bank Inflow Audit Trap" for Gig Workers
Because freelancers and digital creators receive compensation via commercial bank transfers, fintech platforms (Moniepoint, OPay, Kuda), and foreign remittances, **tax authorities classify them exclusively under Direct Assessment**.

During state tax audits:
- State tax intelligence units cross-reference bank transaction histories (via BVN data-sharing frameworks under Section 48 of NTA 2025).
- **Gross Credit Inflow Risk:** Tax inspectors treat **all incoming credit transfers** on a personal bank statement as taxable gross income unless the taxpayer produces verifiable evidence proving otherwise (e.g., inter-account transfers, loan disbursements, personal gifts, or reimbursable business expenses).
- Without structured expense documentation, the SIRS will disallow unbacked expense claims and levy taxes on the entire gross bank inflow, backdating penalties and interest (up to 10% penalty + CBN MPR interest rate).
